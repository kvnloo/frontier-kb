# Justin Sung: encoding quality beats exposure time

## Claim (one sentence)

Higher-order encoding — grouping, comparing, and relating ideas into a schema — produces durable knowledge; rereading, highlighting, and raw lecture hours mostly produce familiarity.

## Evidence

- Sung's public teaching (iCanStudy / YouTube) inverts Bloom's taxonomy for *new* material: start by organizing and evaluating relationships, then retrieve. Retrieval practice is necessary but is not a substitute for encoding a messy source into structure.
- Cognitive-science neighbors (not Sung-original): levels of processing (Craik & Lockhart), desirable difficulty, and schema theory. We cite Sung as the teaching *surface* this vault uses, not as the primary empirical source.

## Fact vs interpretation

- Fact: Sung publicly argues that most students confuse consumption with learning, and that mind maps are schema-construction tools, not decorations.
- Interpretation: a vault UI that dumps full notes first is an anti-pattern. The humanity-vault encode studio must force comparison, grouping, and a one-sentence distilled claim before the full body is shown.
